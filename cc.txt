# Infrastructure and Operations Ownership Transition Program

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the plan, ownership model, milestones, and success criteria for transitioning all infrastructure, platform, security, release management, and operational responsibilities from the current managed services provider to internal teams.

### Transition Objective

By June 30, 2026:

* Internal teams assume primary ownership of all infrastructure and operational processes.
* Internal teams can independently support production environments.
* Internal teams can independently perform deployments and releases.
* Internal teams can independently manage security and compliance activities.
* Vendor transitions to advisory and escalation support only.

# 11. Success Criteria

The transition will be considered successful when:

* All critical documentation has been received.
* All ownership transfers are completed.
* Internal teams have completed reverse KT.
* Internal teams independently perform operational activities.
* Vendor support is limited to advisory assistance.
* No critical production activities require vendor involvement.

Ownership

What remains with the vendor during the 3-month support period?
What remains permanently with the vendor (if anything)?
What is considered "day 1 ownership" vs "later ownership"?

Most Important Decision Questions

If I were leading the meeting, I would insist on getting answers to these first:

Who owns security operations starting July 1?
Who owns production incidents starting July 1?
Who owns AWS administration starting July 1?
Who owns releases starting July 1?
What activities are the vendor still allowed to perform after June 30?
Who signs off that the transition is complete?
What is the fallback plan if a workstream is not ready by June 30?

These seven answers will define the rest of the transition plan and RACI matrix. Without them, the ownership model will remain ambiguous.
